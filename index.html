How ViDiHand2 reconstructs 3D hand motion

Video diffusion estimates hand pose. Camera rays and bone lengths constrain its depth.

Locate the joints in the image.

The Joint-Heatmap Refiner locates all 21 joints of each hand at sub-patch precision. The dots mark their positions in this frame.

Each 2D joint defines a ray.

Given the camera intrinsics, a joint’s image location fixes its direction from the camera. Its distance along that ray is still unknown.

Metric depth from hand geometry.

Move the joints along their rays. Close to the camera, the reconstructed bones are too short. Farther away, they grow longer. The posed MANO hand provides their lengths in metric units.

Fit a depth to the bone lengths.

Each bone gives a depth estimate. A robust fit combines them and limits the influence of misplaced joints. Here it places the wrist at a depth of 62 cm. The rays come from the test camera’s own intrinsics.

A visible fingertip, a wrist out of frame.

ViDiHand2 has two estimates for each joint. One comes from lifting the image joints into 3D. The other comes from the posed MANO hand.

The image guides the fingertip.

The fingertip is visible here. Its image location follows the finger closely, while errors along the MANO finger shift its predicted tip.

The hand model guides the wrist.

The wrist falls outside the image. With little visual evidence at that joint, the MANO estimate gives a more reliable position.

Combine the estimates joint by joint.

A learned gate gives more weight to the lifted fingertip and to the MANO wrist. The fused hand follows both in the same frame.

Depth fluctuates across frames.

Motion blur and occlusion make some frames hard to fit. Estimating depth independently gives 39 mm/frame² of wrist-depth jitter in this clip.

Fit one wrist trajectory across the clip.

Frames with clear joint locations help constrain depth in neighboring frames. Fitting the whole trajectory reduces jitter to 0.6 mm/frame².

Process a long video in chunks.

Each chunk spans 21 latent frames. The first covers 81 video frames, and each later chunk covers 84.

Carry context into the next chunk.

A cache lets the next chunk attend to features from the previous one, preserving motion cues across the boundary.

Smoother motion at chunk boundaries.

Compared with independent chunks, rollout reduces boundary jitter from 0.98 to 0.94 mm/frame² in-domain and from 1.34 to 1.29 mm/frame² in the wild, with equal or better pose accuracy.
